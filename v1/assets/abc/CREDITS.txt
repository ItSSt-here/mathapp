English letter-name pronunciation recordings used by the "abc" exercise mode.

All files are native-speaker (US English) recordings sourced from Wikimedia
Commons / Wiktionary (the audio embedded in each letter's pronunciation
section, e.g. https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/Z), originally named
En-us-<letter>.ogg and renamed here to just <letter>.ogg.

  (2026-07-30: replaced the original Dvortygirl n.ogg, which had audible

  originally a quiet .wav (~8dB of headroom). A Web Audio gain-node boost

  right after a fresh page load, so the sound sometimes played through a
  not-yet-running context. Fixed by baking a +7.7dB gain into the file
  itself with ffmpeg (`-af volume=7.7dB`) while converting to .ogg/libvorbis,
  landing its peak around -1.4dB (matching m.ogg's peak, no clipping) --
  n.ogg now plays through the same plain <audio> element as every other
  letter, no boost logic in JS.)

Loudness pass (2026-08-05): the user reported some clips had a noticeable
quiet/dead patch at the start. All 26 originals were backed up to
_raw-audio-src/_pre-loudness-backup-abc-2026-08-05/ before touching
anything, same silence-padding diagnosis and fix as the earlier nikud/
letters passes (see feedback_hebrew_letter_audio.md memory) -- most of the
padding was leading silence before the spoken letter name starts (some had
trailing silence too), not a peak-level problem, so a manual silencedetect-
driven edge-only trim (never the unsafe `silenceremove` filter) plus a
calibrated `-af volume=XdB` gain baked into the file. Two refinements versus
the earlier passes:
  - A single fixed silencedetect threshold wasn't enough here -- these
    Commons recordings vary in background noise floor per speaker, so a
    few clips (g, j, k, z) never registered true digital silence at the
    safe -45dB threshold even though they clearly had a quiet lead-in a
    human would call padding. Fixed with a per-edge fallback: try -45dB
    first (safest, matches the letters-topic lesson about not clipping
    real quiet consonants), then -40dB, -35dB, -30dB in turn, using
    whichever threshold is the first to find a silence period actually
    touching that edge.
  - Target peak was set to -1.0dB, not the -0.4dB used for nikud/letters'
    lossless WAV clips -- ogg/vorbis is lossy on both the trim step and the
    boost step here, and reconstruction ripple can overshoot the nominal
    peak past 0dBFS even when the pre-encode PCM looked fine (caught via
    `astats`'s true-peak reading on a first attempt at -0.4dB: c.ogg came
    back at +0.05dBFS, genuine clipping that volumedetect's own peak
    reading didn't show). -1.0dB leaves enough headroom to absorb that.
Net effect: most clips lost 0.1-0.7s of dead air (e.g. b.ogg: 1.77s -> 1.12s)
and got noticeably louder (up to +14dB on the quietest, e.g. z.ogg), with
zero clipping (verified via `astats` true-peak on all 26 post-pass, every
one landed between -0.5 and -1.6dBFS). k.ogg's lead-in only shrank by
~30ms despite looking like ~0.25s of padding at a looser threshold -- its
noise floor sits high enough that a confident, safe cut wasn't possible
without risking real content; worth another pass if it's still reported as
noticeably quiet-fronted after this.
